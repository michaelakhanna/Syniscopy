\begin{abstract}
Syniscopy is a shared-scene Fisher-information diagnostic for comparing nanoparticle imaging modalities under explicit forward-model assumptions. The method maps multiple modality renderers onto a common lateral/axial scene parameterization, computes rerendered finite-difference Cramer--Rao lower bounds, and emits status-gated artifacts for modality ranking, detected-quanta normalization, algebraic fusion, and supervision-mask policy checks. The central claim is not a universal benchmark ordering; it is that comparisons are interpretable only after the scene, measurement domain, noise model, and derivative contract are fixed. We demonstrate the workflow on configured optical and electron profile cards, native-regime source-provenance audits, deterministic supervision-policy cases, and a caustic-matched SAM2 worked example. Headline paper artifacts are tied to explicit convergence and validation-status files so failed derivative gates remain visible rather than being hidden in prose.
\end{abstract}
