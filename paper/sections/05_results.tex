\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Results convention.}
All numerical results below are contract-scoped diagnostics. A row is treated as paper-facing evidence only when its emitted convergence and validation-status fields say so; otherwise it remains a diagnostic artifact for model development rather than a benchmark claim. This convention replaces repeated per-paragraph disclaimers.


The results are organized around the analyses supporting claims C1--C5. First, forward renders and composite-particle examples show one shared scene represented across multiple modality profiles. Second, Cram\'{e}r--Rao orderings, fusion diagnostics, and Fisher density maps evaluate the information layer. Third, supervision-schema evaluations and a Segment Anything Model 2 transfer example show how supervision masks and auxiliary arrays can support a downstream model.

\paragraph{Closed-form scaling checks.} The lateral Cram\'{e}r--Rao implementation includes checks against three expected laws: $\sigma_{xy}\propto 1/\mathrm{SNR}$ at fixed point-spread-function width, $\sigma_{xy}\propto d^{-3}$ for a controlled Rayleigh-amplitude response, and diameter-independence at fixed signal-to-noise ratio \cite{thompson-larson-webb,ober-ram-ward,juffmann-iscat-cobri-crlb}. \Cref{tab:closed-form-scaling-checks} reports the fitted exponent or residual for each check. These are software consistency diagnostics under controlled inputs, not empirical validation of microscope performance. The Rayleigh row holds the image geometry and noise fixed while scaling contrast amplitude as $d^3$, so the Fisher bound should scale as $d^{-3}$. The fixed-SNR diameter-independence check is a renormalized control in which the signal amplitude is held fixed as diameter changes; both controls are separate from the particle-size sweep in \cref{fig:particle-size-sweep}, where modality-specific contrast amplitude, sampling, and noise can vary with diameter and the Cram\'{e}r--Rao bound is therefore expected to deviate from a closed-form law.

\paragraph{Comparison contracts.} Table-to-table claims are contract-specific in this section. We use four explicit contracts: Contract-LP (configured-profile lateral Cram\'{e}r--Rao), Contract-LZ (configured-profile axial/\(\mathrm{SE}(3)\) diagnostics), Contract-Q (detected-quanta-normalized comparison), and Contract-NR (native-regime source-use context). The same profile can therefore produce different orderings under different contracts.

Cross-modality Cram\'{e}r--Rao lower bounds in \Cref{tab:modality-ranking} are Contract-LP diagnostics: they are reported on a shared physical coordinate frame with per-modality detector pitch and noise profiles, not as a native-instrument comparison. Bench mapping is approximate: the current configuration parameters do not absorb all stray light, alignment error, out-of-focus particles, dust, or detector nonlinearity; source-matching residuals are addressed explicitly through configurable sample-environment perturbations including \texttt{sample\_environment\_pattern\_roughness\_model}, \texttt{sample\_environment\_pattern\_roughness\_amplitude}, \texttt{sample\_environment\_pattern\_roughness\_correlation\_pixels}, \texttt{sample\_environment\_pattern\_roughness\_phase\_std}, and \texttt{sample\_environment\_pattern\_roughness\_source} (for \texttt{source\_matched} mode), with roughness-source coupling options \texttt{independent}, \texttt{coherent\_amplitude}, \texttt{field\_weighted}, \texttt{scene\_weighted}, and \texttt{channel\_weighted} (default: \texttt{channel\_weighted}). The transmission-electron-microscopy row uses its configured backend-specific detector, dose, and potential/contrast settings \texttt{ctf\_proxy}/\texttt{multislice\_lite}/\texttt{syniscopy\_multislice}; native transmission-electron-microscopy sampling remains a separate instrument-specific configuration in Contract-NR.

\Cref{tab:modality-ranking} reports the default Contract-LP configured-profile diagnostic bound and within-profile relative bound. Under the default production-grid calculation, the lowest lateral bounds are scanning electron microscopy secondary-electron, interferometric scattering microscopy, and off-axis digital holography; rows that fail the convergence gate should not be used for precise numerical claims without rerunning the intended derivative-step and detector-grid choices. The differential-phase-contrast row is reported via the configured asymmetric-illumination phase-gradient model (vectorial Debye, defaulting to \texttt{full\_vector} non-reduced vectorial detection, with optional \texttt{analyzer\_x}, \texttt{analyzer\_y}, \texttt{incoherent\_sum}, \texttt{unpolarized}, and scalar \texttt{two-axis} options), and metadata records the selected backend.

\paragraph{Detected-quanta normalization.} Contract-Q assigns each count-domain modality the same detected-quanta budget and evaluates the resulting count image with a Poisson-Gaussian mean-Fisher diagnostic. The Contract-Q output now emits a dedicated rerendered derivative-convergence artifact, \Cref{tab:detected-quanta-derivative-convergence}, so the fixed-budget ranking is status-tagged by its own Contract-Q convergence export rather than relying only on prose-level inheritance from Contract-LP. This is a common-budget diagnostic, not a claim that photons, electrons, reference-arm counts, and sample damage constraints are interchangeable operating costs. Quantitative phase imaging remains phase-domain and is evaluated through the phase-noise model, so its row is model-conditional rather than an intrinsic statement about all phase-imaging instruments. Because several count-domain rows in \Cref{tab:detected-quanta-normalization-metadata} have readout fractions materially away from zero (including 0.424 and 0.567), this scaling check is interpreted as a diagnostic reference rather than an exact model for every point in the reported sweep. \Cref{tab:budget-normalized} summarizes the fixed-budget comparison, and \cref{fig:detected-quanta-budget-pareto} shows the sweep over detected-quanta budgets. Contract-LP and Contract-Q answer different questions: the former uses propagated detector noise under representative modality profiles, while the latter maps eligible modalities to a fixed detected-quanta budget and Poisson/phase noise model. Signed-contrast and reference-arm modalities can move between these normalizations when a small contrast rides on a bright Poisson background. Dose, bleaching, radiation damage, heating, preparation, and destruction penalties belong to the acquisition-cost metadata layer and can be included when that contract is configured.

\paragraph{Fusion Cram\'{e}r--Rao bound.} Best-$k$ subset search reports the lowest-bound single modality, the lowest-bound subset at each size, and full-library fusion in \cref{tab:fusion-crlb,fig:fusion-curve}. For these reported curves, Contract-LP is the underlying modality contract unless explicitly stated; any subset containing a non-passing row in Table~\ref{tab:derivative-convergence-crlb} inherits that row-level diagnostic status. The fusion bound is monotonically non-increasing with subset size under independent noise Fisher additivity. A lateral registration covariance can be supplied to model subpixel cross-channel alignment error before summing Fisher matrices; \cref{tab:registration-degradation} reports the corresponding synthetic degradation check. The full-library row is an algebraic independent-channel diagnostic, not a proposed physically simultaneous instrument. In experimental design, users should only fuse modalities that are independently acquired, mutually compatible with the same sample state, and not alternate reconstructions of the same detected quanta.

\subsection{Caustic-matched SAM2 worked example}

The Segment Anything Model 2 transfer example uses the Liverpool caustic-particle dataset \cite{schleyer-caustic} and Segment Anything Model 2 \cite{sam2}. Segment Anything Model 2 is fine-tuned on the synthetic corpus above, then the base and fine-tuned models are run on the same reviewed set of Liverpool 50~nm caustic-particle clips with the same prompt records for each clip. The candidate pool is restricted to the defined Liverpool 50~nm source condition, and 30 videos are selected at random from that pool for review. Candidate videos are then screened before inference using promptability and data-quality criteria specified before model comparison. A retained clip must support one stable target identity, one point prompt on the selected prompt frame, and a contiguous evaluation window in which the same target remains interpretable for at least 30 frames. The review criterion is therefore window-level rather than frame-level: exclusion does not mean that no individual frame contains visible particle signal, but that the clip does not provide a well-defined prompt-to-trajectory comparison window. Videos are excluded before model inference when manual review cannot assign that target and window with confidence because the particle is ambiguous or absent, leaves or contacts the field boundary, is affected by severe acquisition artifacts, or otherwise cannot support one prompt record for one particle. Of the 30 reviewed candidates, 19 were retained (3 at 2.5\% FBS, 4 at 5\% FBS, and 12 at 10\% FBS) and 11 were excluded by these pre-inference criteria. No manual segmentation masks from the Liverpool videos are used for training or scoring; manual review supplies only the retained clip window and prompt point.

The transfer analysis is populated from the retained rows of the review manifest rather than a hand-coded single clip, and the E09 batch manifest records per-clip prompt manifests, continuity semantics, and mask-output paths when the inference notebook is run. For each retained clip, the local review records store the source DataCat filename, prompt frame, prompt coordinates, selected end frame, encoded clip path, 0.116~$\mu$m pixel size, and the DataCat 40~fps acquisition timebase \cite{schleyer-caustic}. The source DataCat README records 40~fps acquisition; the retained AVI files play back at 5~fps for review and notebook visualization, so Brownian quantities use the 40~fps acquisition interval recorded by the dataset. The source article describes positively charged BPEI-coated 50~nm gold particles in DMEM with fetal bovine serum, a 37~$^\circ$C incubation stage, 100-frame tracks, and diffusion computed from two-dimensional mean-squared displacement \cite{schleyer2024label}. The overlay panel in \cref{fig:sam2-brownian-transfer} and mask-centroid diffusion summary in \cref{tab:sam2-transfer-motion} show how Syniscopy supervision changes downstream video masks on the same retained experimental clips. The primary transfer effect is mask continuity: the fine-tuned outputs retain the prompted mask across the full reviewed window in all three serum groups, while the base model loses continuity in at least part of each group. The grouped mask-centroid diffusion diagnostic is secondary. It moves closer to the source-reference diffusion means in the 2.5\% and 5\% FBS groups and moves farther from the reference in the 10\% group, so it is reported as a condition-level diagnostic rather than a uniform diffusion-accuracy claim. The group counts are small for 2.5\% and 5\% FBS, so these grouped differences are interpreted with the reported standard deviations and continuity column rather than as precise subgroup diffusion estimates. Because the source study reports diffusion coefficients rather than a per-track diameter ground truth, the summary reports mask-centroid diffusion together with mask coverage and trajectory continuity rather than forcing a Stokes--Einstein diameter inversion.

This transfer result is therefore an existence-and-continuity demonstration for the Syniscopy supervision pipeline under matched review conditions, not a statistically powered benchmark of video-segmentation performance on Liverpool clips.
